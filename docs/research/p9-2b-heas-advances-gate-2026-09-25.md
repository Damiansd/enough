## 1. How much, what period, interaction with the fortnightly loan
- Cap: **"You can advance up to 50% of the maximum pension rate of your qualifying pension in any 26 fortnight period."** (advance-payments page). Confirmed **ANNUAL**, not fortnightly, by the Olli example: *"The maximum annual pension amount for a partnered person is $23,101.00. This means Olli can get a maximum advance amount of $11,550.50."* $11,550.50 = 0.5 × $23,101.00 (annual). This matches repo's T33 / `heasAdvanceCap` (`ADVANCE_CAP_MULTIPLE × MPR_fortnightly × 26`) — **T33 is correct, not part of the live ambiguity.**
- Max **2 advances per rolling 26-fortnight period**: *"you can't get more than 2 advance payments in any 26 fortnight period"* (advance-payments page); s 1134A(2)(b) AustLII text: *"the person has not received more than one pension loans scheme advance payment in relation to a ... period commencing during the previous 26 fortnights."*
- Period definition: **rolls from the day the FIRST advance in the window was worked out, per Person** — s 1133AA(1): *"'pension loans scheme advance payment period', for a person, means the period of 26 fortnights starting on the day in relation to which the person's pension loans scheme advance payment was worked out under subsection 1134A(2)."* Matches repo's `advancePeriodFortnights` note verbatim.
- Interaction: **YES, it reduces the fortnightly loan for the rest of the 26-fortnight window**, smoothed (divided by 26) — see §2. *"Any advance payment taken may reduce the fortnightly loan amount you can get for a period of 26 fortnights. You can always top-up your fortnightly payment to 100% of your maximum pension rate."* (advance-payments page — this "100% of MPR" floor is the same floor as s 1134(1A)(b)(i)'s "greater of the maximum payment rate and ...").

## 2. Resolving §5.3's ambiguity
Register §5.3 gave two candidate expressions:
- **Table row** (as literally quoted): `max(MPR, 1.5 × MPR − advances_taken)` — cites s 1134(1A)(b).
- **Pseudocode**: `heas_fn_during_advance = max(0, (heas_headroom_annual − advances_in_period) / 26)`.

**The pseudocode is right; the table row's formula, read literally (as a single un-annualised subtraction), is wrong and must be corrected, not implemented as written.**

AustLII's actual text of s 1134(1A)(b)(i) is: *"the greater of the maximum payment rate and 1.5 multiplied by the maximum payment rate less any amounts of pension loans scheme advance payment received by the person in relation to the period."* Read with no annualising step, this subtracts a whole lump-sum dollar figure from a *fortnightly* rate (1.5×MPR is fortnightly) — for George below that would floor his rate at bare MPR for the full 26 fortnights, forfeiting **all** $15,323.10 of his annual loan entitlement for a $10,000 advance. That is not what happens.

Services Australia's own worked example ("Example 1 — George") settles it, cent-for-cent, in favour of the annualised-then-divided-by-26 reading:
> *"George also gets the maximum $589.35 ($15,323.10 yearly) rate of loan payments... he wants a lump sum payment of $10,000... $15,323.10 yearly − $10,000 (advance payment) = $5,323.10 divided by 26 = $204.73 for 26 fortnights. George gets the combined fortnightly payment of $1,383.43."*

$1,178.70 (pension) + $204.73 = $1,383.43 — exact match. This is **exactly** `max(0, (heas_headroom_annual − advances_in_period) / 26)` with `heas_headroom_annual = (1.5×MPR_fn − pension_fn) × 26 = $15,323.10`. **Verdict: implement the register's pseudocode line. Correct or delete the table row — it is a true statement of s 1134(1A)(b)'s words only if you also silently annualise `1.5 × MPR` and divide the whole right-hand side by 26, which the table row as transcribed does not show and which a reader/implementer will get wrong (a 26× error in George's favour, i.e. the customer would appear to lose their entire year's loan top-up for a $10,000 advance instead of $384.62/ft).** The "greater of MPR" floor is real (confirmed independently by "You can always top-up your fortnightly payment to 100% of your maximum pension rate") but binds at the **combined pension+loan** level, i.e. `max(pension_fn, pension_fn + heas_fn_during_advance)` collapses to the pseudocode's `max(0, …)` on the loan-only component whenever `pension_fn ≥ MPR_fn` (full-rate pensioners, both worked examples). **UNCONFIRMED**: how the floor behaves for a genuine part-rate pensioner whose `pension_fn < MPR_fn` — neither Services Australia example nor the AustLII text disambiguates whether the Act's floor lifts such a person's *combined* payment up to full MPR during a heavy-advance period (a windfall) or floors only the *loan* component at zero. Flag before generalising past the two published examples (both are full-rate-or-nil cases).

## 3. Age Pension treatment of advances
Confirmed, and matches register §5.7 exactly (already `primary-verified` there — no change needed):
> *"Advance payments of loans under the scheme may impact your rate of pension because they're both: subject to deeming provisions under the income test [and] assessable under the assets test... If you used your principal home as security for the loan the advance won't count for the assets test for the first 90 days."* — advance-payments page.

So: **deemed as a financial asset from day one** (income test), **assessable under the assets test after 90 days**, exempt for those 90 days **only** where the home was the security. Register's `HEAS_ADVANCE_ASSET_EXEMPT_DAYS = 90` is corroborated, not newly resolved by this task.

## 4. Interest on advances
Same 3.95% p.a., compounding fortnightly, from the day the advance is paid: *"We add the advance payment to your loan balance when we pay it to you. The loan balance accrues interest on a fortnightly basis."* (advance-payments page) and s 1135(3) Method statement Step 1A ("Add to the primary loan amount the amount of any pension loans scheme advance payments received by the person") sits **before** Step 4 (interest), confirming advances join the balance before that fortnight's interest is charged — the same order T40 already established for ordinary draws. No separate rate or compounding rule for advances.
